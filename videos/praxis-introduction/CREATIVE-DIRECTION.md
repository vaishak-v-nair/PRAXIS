# PRAXIS — Looks finished. Let us check.

Creative direction, 2026-10-03. This is a product introduction for the real,
local-first PRAXIS tool, using the visual craft of a SaaS launch film.

## Three directions considered

1. Product with a quiet mascot: a memorable original opening and ending, with
   real demonstrations carrying the middle. Recommended because the viewer
   understands what PRAXIS does and sees it work.
2. Mascot-led adventure: the axolotl travels through a simulated code world.
   Fun, but risks hiding the product and implying capabilities that are not real.
3. Abstract engine film: light, paths and technical typography. Polished, but
   interchangeable with other AI products and less clear to a teenager.

## Selected story

A builder has made an app with AI. It looks finished. Our axolotl looks closer.
A project outline opens into the actual website. The browser check gives a
private first look; the local tool performs the deeper review. Four specialists
and the human share context. The turning point is a real test: the broken app
returns success but saves no order. A readable finding becomes a plan the
builder can hand to their coding agent. We return to the mascot and PRAXIS.

The cause-and-effect arc is: project → inspect → test → understand → plan.
The mascot conveys curiosity and warmth, not an autonomous proof of correctness.
The demo shows a deliberate correct/broken fixture pair, not customer results.

## Original motion vocabulary

- Opening: real mascot on a crisp pixel plinth beside a newly drawn project
  outline. Three source sheets align into one project and a lens path opens.
  The question is a single short title, separate from subtitles.
- Handoffs: a thin project corner line carries into the next shot at the same
  coordinates. It remains outside the product recording. No text-bearing
  crossfades, glitch, badge pile, star symbol or decorative status indicator.
- Proof: hold the real Docker PASS and FAIL logs long enough to read. A failed
  backend side effect never becomes a success through animation or narration.
- Ending: the happy source animation accompanies a large official wordmark,
  the website URL and one clear browser-first/local-next call to action.

## Motion and hierarchy

1920×1080, 30fps. Retain the 124.69-second conversational ElevenLabs Chris
narration already generated; no robotic SAPI fallback and no fabricated human
actor claim. Use measured speech timing, quiet edit boundaries and sentence
captions. Title zone above the recording; subtitles own y=920–1024. Animation
never covers the controls, actual result, caption band or reading target.

Use neutral navy, off-white, restrained product blue and the mascot's peach.
Local Inter and JetBrains Mono. Actual app colors remain intact. Rehearsed,
real Playwright footage is cut for pacing; raw takes and edit ranges are kept.
No recreated app UI, invented numbers, fake clicks or automatic repairs.

## Reference research and selected toolkits

Nate Herk's linked X post resolves to an article on narration-led editing:
https://x.com/nateherk/status/2097449583811125739
The primary post metadata and article preview are saved locally. The matching
creator's tutorial is https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o3IEkKXXXvo . Its applicable
workflow is transcribe → cut → plan → build → verify, and preserve reusable
feedback. We borrow the process, not footage, effects or the creator's layout.

https://github.com/zhuyansen/awesome-claude-video-skills is a catalogue, not a
single installable skill. A pinned local copy is kept under .tools. We reviewed
and installed saas-motion-video and business-motion-film, with their referenced
text/scripts. They contribute tone, composition variety, component invention
and independent criticism. Hyperframes remains the renderer. No community MCP
is registered as an evidence source; PRAXIS dependencies and hooks are unchanged.
The catalogue's SAFE label is a README assessment, not a dependency audit.

The SaaS kit's UI-recreation option is intentionally not used here: user
requirements call for real execution and real product recordings. The film
uses cut/match transitions as an intentional reading motif. It is a tutorial
as well as an introduction; purposeful proof-reading holds beat arbitrary
motion quotas. No existing private brand masters will be published.

## Acceptance and QA

Check frame-zero composition, chapter midpoints, dense transitions, pixel bounds,
text contrast, subtitle timing/containment, real video playback, seek consistency,
readable PASS/FAIL, clean decode, duration, audio clipping and source privacy.
A fresh critic sees rendered pixels and the user's brief. Record what was
sampled versus measured; no claim of exhaustive listening or award status.
